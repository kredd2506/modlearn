Was it the chip or the software?

Started September 2026 · in progress

A 2025 UCSD study (arXiv:2507.00418) served 12 LLMs on the Qualcomm Cloud AI 100 Ultra and on 4×/8× NVIDIA A100, using vLLM on both. It found competitive energy efficiency, a lead in tokens per watt on 3 of 12 models, and much finer hardware allocation: a small model on one 36 W device, a 70B model on one card. Its tables also show headroom that looks like software: similar models needing very different amounts of silicon, and batching capped at 4.

Since then, Qualcomm acquired Modular, and ModCon 2026 showed MAX running on Cloud AI 100 Ultra. This project re-runs the study with MAX as the serving engine to measure how much better the chip gets with a newer software stack.

Hypotheses, stated before any runs:

  • H1: on Qualcomm, MAX substantially raises throughput over the paper's numbers at similar power
  • H2: on the A100, MAX matches or beats vLLM in throughput and tok/s/W
  • H3: a right-sized A100 (1 GPU for ≤8B models) raises the A100's tokens per watt
  • H4: one Mojo kernel runs unchanged on Apple, NVIDIA, and Qualcomm silicon
